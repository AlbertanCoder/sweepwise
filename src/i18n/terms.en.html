5. What Sweepwise changes.

(a) Sweepwise never permanently deletes Your Files. When the Tester approves moving or removing them, they are moved, or sent to the Recycle Bin (Windows) or Trash (Mac and Linux), and History & Undo can put them back. Sweepwise never empties the Recycle Bin or Trash.

(b) Email the Tester approves is moved to the mailbox's Trash. Sweepwise never empties Trash.

(c) Temporary Files are deleted permanently. The first time, Sweepwise shows the list and waits until the Tester presses Clean now, and it always skips anything changed in the last 24 hours (on Windows, also anything created in the last 24 hours). If the Tester switches on weekly auto-clean (off unless switched on), it repeats only this cleaning, and each one appears in History.

(d) Deep Clean runs only when the Tester presses it, and never touches Your Files or empties the Recycle Bin or Trash. On Windows it uses Windows' own Disk Cleanup and repair tools and never touches the Downloads folder; on Mac it removes system log archives and crash reports older than two weeks; on Linux it removes downloaded package files, system logs older than two weeks and old, switched-off snap versions.

(e) Programs are removed only if the Tester ticks them: on Windows with each program's own uninstaller, and on Linux through Flatpak or Snap, and Undo cannot bring those back; on Mac the app is moved to the Trash, and Undo can bring it back. Folders that removed programs left behind are offered unticked and go to the Recycle Bin or Trash if the Tester ticks them. On Windows, the old copy of Windows left after an upgrade ("Windows.old") is offered only 10 or more days after the upgrade and removed only if the Tester ticks it; Undo cannot bring it back.

(f) Speed Boost (Windows and Linux) changes only settings the Tester approves, such as the power plan and visual effects (on Linux, the power profile and animations), and records the old values so Undo can put them back. Programs that start with the computer are switched off only if the Tester approves it, in a way that can be switched back on.

(g) Before removing programs, changing the power settings or visual effects, or installing app updates, Sweepwise asks for a safety copy of the system: a restore point on Windows, a Time Machine local snapshot on Mac, or a Timeshift snapshot on Linux when Timeshift is installed. If none can be made, those changes wait. On Windows and Mac, Deep Clean also asks for one first and reports whether it worked.
